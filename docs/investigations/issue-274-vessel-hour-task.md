## Bounded outcome

Implement one committed hour of travel aboard one of the seven large vehicles recorded by #2074, on top of the #144 one-hour travel segment. This task owns the PDF100–101 Large Vehicles notes on speed, crew, passengers and cargo. For the seven table vehicles it also owns two sentences that lie inside the page range of the open #150 split (PDF12–13, 192–201): the PDF12 sentence that characters in a waterborne vessel are limited to the vessel's speed and choose no pace, and the PDF192 Vehicles paragraph that sends travelers to the vehicle's speed in "Equipment". Decision #274 records this carve-out; #150's split must not publish a second owner for them. Land-vehicle pace and the doubled mounted distance stay with #150. The vessel instance, with its identity, ownership and current Hit Points, is owned by #2078; this task reads it and adds position, crew roster, passengers and cargo for the hour. Damage and repair belong to #2078. Hiring and paying a crew belong to the #275 economy split.

Speed: travelers aboard use the vessel's table speed in miles per hour and do not choose a travel pace, so no pace modifier applies. Speeds and distances are exact rationals. The PDF5 Round Down rule is not applied to a vessel speed: the PDF101 table itself prints 1½ and 2½ miles per hour, so a vessel speed is not a whole-number quantity, and the rounded value would change with the unit. A halved Warship speed is 1¼ miles per hour, not 1. Keelboats and Rowboats are used on lakes and rivers. For those two, going downstream adds the current's speed; the current must be a declared authoritative fact, and the typical 3 miles per hour is offered only as an explicit GM choice. They cannot be rowed against any significant current. The source attaches the current rule to those two vessels, so a current on any other vessel is unavailable. Being pulled upstream by draft animals has no source speed and stays unavailable.

Wind and propulsion: PDF100 says "A ship sailing against a strong wind moves at half speed" and "In a dead calm (no wind), waterborne ships can't move under sail and must be rowed." The source does not say which of the seven rows sail. Whether a vessel is under sail or rowed for the hour is therefore an authoritative declared fact, needed only when the declared wind is strong and against, or a dead calm. Under sail against a strong wind, any waterborne vessel moves at exactly half its table speed. The source treats the Keelboat and Rowboat as rowed vessels ("These vehicles can't be rowed against any significant current"), so a rowed Keelboat or Rowboat hour uses its table speed, is not halved by wind and is available in a dead calm. The source gives no rowed speed for the Galley, Longship, Sailing Ship or Warship, so a rowed hour for those four, and therefore any dead-calm hour, is unavailable. Under a strong opposing wind with no declared propulsion the hour is unavailable. The Airship is not waterborne and the source does not say whether it sails; an Airship hour against a strong wind or in a dead calm is unavailable, and with neither declared it moves 8 miles.

Crew: a ship larger than a Keelboat or Rowboat needs at least its table number of skilled hirelings to function. Count only authoritative crew members recorded as skilled hirelings through the output that the #275 split assigns; until that output is qualified, the five crewed ships stay unavailable. The native #275 edge is satisfied when that planning issue closes, so before it closes its split must add its skilled-hireling child as a native blocker of this task. The Keelboat and Rowboat need their crew of one and no hireling.

Passengers: the table number is Small and Medium passengers in hammocks. A ship outfitted with private accommodations carries one-fifth as many, rounded down (PDF5), because passengers are counted whole. The outfitting is an authoritative ship fact. The usual 5 SP hammock and 2 GP cabin prices are records only; no fare is charged here.

Cargo: the table gives cargo in tons. Declared cargo must be an authoritative amount in tons. The source does not define a ton in pounds, so converting item weights to tons is unavailable.

Hours: PDF12 says ships might be able to travel up to 24 hours per day depending on the vessel and crew. Hours beyond the campaign's ordinary travel day need an explicit GM-declared continuous-sailing fact, and a 25th hour in one day always rejects.

## Deterministic fixtures

- Sailing Ship, 20 skilled hirelings, no adverse wind: one hour moves 2 miles and offers no pace. Declared under sail against a strong wind it moves 1 mile; with no declared propulsion that hour is unavailable. In a dead calm the hour is unavailable, with the missing rowed speed named as the reason. With 19 skilled hirelings, or 20 crew of whom one is untrained, it cannot sail.
- Warship: 2½ miles, and exactly 1¼ miles under sail against a strong wind; a result of 1 mile from rounding down is rejected. Longship under sail against a strong wind: 1½ miles. Galley: 80 crew required; 79 rejects; any passenger rejects because its passenger entry is absent.
- Keelboat, crew 1, declared 3 mph current downstream: 4 miles. With no declared current the downstream hour is unavailable and never assumes 3. Upstream against a significant current rejects. Rowboat, crew 1, still water: 1½ miles.
- Rowboat, rowed: 1½ miles against a strong wind and 1½ miles in a dead calm. Rowboat declared under sail: ¾ mile against a strong wind and rejected in a dead calm. Keelboat, rowed, dead calm: 1 mile. Airship against a strong wind or in a dead calm: unavailable; with neither declared, 8 miles.
- Passengers: Longship 150 in hammocks, 151 rejects; with private accommodations 30, and 31 rejects. Sailing Ship 20 and 4. Keelboat 6 and 1. Rowboat 3 and 0.
- Cargo: Sailing Ship 100 tons commits and 100½ rejects. Keelboat ½ ton commits. Rowboat and any declared cargo tonnage rejects because its cargo entry is absent. Cargo given only in pounds is unavailable.
- Negative, each violated independently: a Fast or Slow pace aboard; a pace Disadvantage or Advantage aboard; halving for a wind that is not strong or not against; halving a rowed Keelboat or Rowboat; a rowed hour for the Galley, Longship, Sailing Ship or Warship; a mounted or land-vehicle segment routed through this task; an unknown route distance; a foreign vessel; a vessel instance created by this task instead of read from #2078; a stale clock or route revision.
