## Bounded outcome

Implement two PDF101 rules for the seven large vehicles recorded by {{274:records}}: the Damage Threshold note, using the PDF180 glossary rule, and Ship Repair. This task resolves no attack against a vessel's AC, no travel, no crew rule and no purchase.

State ownership: this task owns the persisted vessel instance for the #274 cohort: campaign, identity, the {{274:records}} row it instantiates, the owning party or character, and current Hit Points up to the table maximum. {{274:voyage}} reads this instance and must not create its own. A vessel enters a campaign only as an explicit isolated or GM-declared fact. Buying a vessel stays unavailable until the #275 split publishes a payment owner, and no task states any other way to acquire one.

Damage threshold: this task consumes one already-resolved damage instance from a single attack or effect, with its amount and type. A vessel with a damage threshold takes the whole instance when the amount equals or exceeds the threshold and takes nothing when it is lower. Compare each instance separately; never add instances together to reach the threshold. A vessel whose threshold entry is absent takes every instance in full. Hit Points do not go below 0 (PDF16). PDF12 says a vehicle is not an object, so the Poison and Psychic immunity of objects is not applied by inference; an instance of either type against a vessel is unavailable without an authoritative ruling. PDF100–101 states no consequence for a vessel at 0 Hit Points, so record the value and invent no sinking, crew or cargo effect.

Ship repair: repairs can be made only while the vessel is berthed, an authoritative fact. Repairing 1 Hit Point takes 1 day and costs 20 GP for materials and labor. Where supplies and skilled labor are abundant, such as a city shipyard, also an authoritative fact, the time and cost are halved: half a day and 10 GP per Hit Point. The halved time is kept exact and the PDF5 Round Down rule is not applied to it: rounding half a day down would make a 1 Hit Point shipyard repair take no time, which is not a halved repair time, so 15 Hit Points take 7½ days, not 7. The halved cost is a whole 10 GP. Confirm an explicit whole number of Hit Points, no more than the missing amount. Commit only completed units: clock advance, GP cost and restored Hit Points together. The GP cost is paid through the coin output that the #275 economy split assigns; until that output is qualified, repair stays unavailable, and a free or unpaid repair never commits. The native #275 edge is satisfied when that planning issue closes, so before it closes its split must add its coin-payment child as a native blocker of this task. No ability check, tool, crew or separate hireling wage is added.

## Deterministic fixtures

- Sailing Ship, 300 HP, threshold 15: an instance of 14 leaves 300; an instance of 15 leaves 285; two instances of 10 in one turn leave 300. Keelboat, threshold 10: 9 leaves 100 and 11 leaves 89. Rowboat, 50 HP, no threshold: an instance of 1 leaves 49. An Airship takes an instance of 1 in full.
- Negative, each violated independently: treating an instance equal to the threshold as superficial; subtracting the threshold from the damage; summing instances; a threshold on the Rowboat or Airship; HP below 0; an automatic sinking at 0; Poison or Psychic damage reduced to 0 by assumption.
- Repair: a berthed Sailing Ship at 285 of 300 repairs 15 HP for 15 days and 300 GP. In a shipyard the same repair takes 7½ days and 150 GP. Repairing 1 HP in a shipyard takes half a day and 10 GP. Reject a repair at sea or with an unknown berth, 16 HP when 15 are missing, a fractional Hit Point, a payer with 299 GP for the 300 GP repair, halving only the time or only the cost, a shipyard halving without the authoritative abundance fact, a 15 HP shipyard repair rounded down to 7 days, and a 1 HP shipyard repair rounded down to 0 days.
- Instance: a declared Sailing Ship instance starts at 300 of 300 HP and survives reload. Reject a second instance with the same identity, a foreign campaign's vessel, an instance whose row is not one of the seven records, and a vessel that appears through purchase.
